CABLE TAPPING
WIRETAP · NSA PRISM · FIBER TAPS
ATTACK #1 PASSIVE
HOW IT WORKS
01
Physical access to fiber optic cable or copper wire — could be underground, in a telecom exchange, or undersea.
02
A vampire tap / optical splitter is clamped on. Fiber taps bend the cable slightly — light leaks out. The tap reads it. Zero packets dropped.
03
All passing data is mirrored to attacker's system. Completely passive — no anomalies, no logs, no alerts. Victim never knows.
04
NSA PRISM / GCHQ TEMPORA: fiber taps on transatlantic cables captured 3 days of global internet traffic — every day. Revealed by Snowden in 2013.
🛡 DEFEND
End-to-end encryption (TLS/E2E) · Physical conduit locks · Optical loss monitoring · Tamper-evident seals on cable runs
FIBER OPTICAL TAP
Near-undetectable. No packets dropped.
Bending fiber by just 1–2° leaks enough light for a tap. Standard monitoring won't catch it. Only optical power meters show the anomaly.

SIGNAL JAMMING
GPS · DRONE KILL · ELECTRONIC WARFARE
ATTACK #2 ACTIVE
JAMMING DIAGRAM
01
Jammer broadcasts high-power RF noise on the same frequency as GPS (1.575 GHz) or target comms band. Legal limit is ~0.1W. Jammers use 10–100W.
02
Victim device's receiver is flooded with noise. Signal-to-noise ratio collapses. GPS lock lost. Drone loses positioning. Falls immediately.
03
Advanced variant: GPS spoofing — broadcast fake GPS coordinates. Drone thinks it's somewhere else. Can be steered into a net or hostile territory.
04
Ukraine 2019–2023: Russian Krasukha-4 jammers disrupted NATO drone ops, civilian aviation GPS, and emergency comms across 50km+ radius.
🛡 DEFEND
Frequency hopping · Directional antennas · Inertial navigation backup · Anti-jam GPS receivers · Faraday shielding

EVIL USB DROP
BAITING ATTACK · OPERATION BUCKSHOT YANKEE
ATTACK #3 BAITING
ANATOMY OF A DROP ATTACK
01
Attacker loads USB with autorun malware and drops it in a target parking lot, café, or lobby. Cost: $5. Effort: 60 seconds.
02
60% of people who find an unknown USB drive plug it into their computer. (University of Illinois study, 2016. Military bases: even higher.)
03
On plug-in: payload auto-executes via HID spoofing (USB pretends to be a keyboard) or autorun. Reverse shell opens in under 3 seconds. AV: zero detections.
04
2008 Buckshot Yankee: Agent.btz worm spread from one USB across SIPRNET — the classified US military network. NSA said it was their worst-ever breach.
🛡 DEFEND
USB port locks · Disable autorun (GPO) · USB whitelist by hardware ID · Training: never plug unknown devices · HID attack detection

ATTACK #4 INLINE
INLINE KEYLOGGER
01
Device is plugged inline between keyboard and computer port — takes 5 seconds to install. Looks like a USB pass-through dongle. No tools needed.
02
Every keystroke passes through the logger before reaching the OS. Completely transparent — the OS sees a normal keyboard. No drivers. No software footprint.
03
Keystrokes stored in internal flash memory (up to 2GB). Attacker returns later to retrieve it, or advanced models transmit over Wi-Fi / GSM in real-time.
04
2014 Eastern Europe: hardware keyloggers found in ATMs at upscale hotel lobbies. Thousands of PINs and card numbers harvested. No antivirus caught it.
🛡 DEFEND
Physical inspection of all ports · USB port locks · Tamper-evident seals · BIOS USB boot protection · Anti-tamper chassis sensors · Virtual keyboard for PINs
